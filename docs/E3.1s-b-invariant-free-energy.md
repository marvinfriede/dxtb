# E3.1s-b — invariant free-energy spectral closure

E3.1s-b completes the dxtb-local invariant spectral path. Functional SCF
free-energy corrections now use a weighted spectral matrix rather than
derivatives through reporting eigenvectors or individually ordered orbital
energies.

For smeared Fermi filling, the matrix represents
`kT [f log(f) + (1-f) log(1-f)]` by a fixed tensor quadrature of the matrix
Fermi function. It consumes the compact `_FermiCoreState` retained by the
selected spectral evaluation: the chemical-potential reference and offset,
channel classifications, structural orbital mask, and Fermi status. It does
not run another primal Fermi search. Equal partitioning distributes the
overlap-weighted trace across real atoms; atomic partitioning uses Mulliken
populations of the same AO matrix. Thus both partitions conserve the same
total correction.

For positive temperatures below the smearing cutoff, the existing clamped
Aufbau occupation and free-energy convention remain in force, including
fractional frontier occupations. With no Fermi settings, the correction
remains exactly zero. Valid empty/full Fermi channels retain the legacy
finite-temperature entropy floor as a constant active-space matrix; invalid or
unconverged channels retain a zero diagnostic matrix. Low-temperature
projector ranks are clamped before the frontier matrix is evaluated, and a
zero fractional frontier weight reuses the lower rank so empty degenerate
spaces do not enter an unnecessary interior projector solve. The private
Fermi state is fixed-shape tensor data and is selected alongside the
corresponding spectral result through the solver.

The functional reporting eigensystem now always uses the ordinary fixed
forward `storch.eighb` configuration. No functional SCF free-energy or
density derivative selects spectral semantics from `requires_grad`; reporting
coefficients and orbital energies do not own these physical derivatives.

Validation on PyTorch 2.14.1 CPU:

- `test_invariant_free_energy.py`: 26 passed. This includes saturated
  low-temperature and finite-temperature empty/full channels, legacy entropy
  floor parity, temperature response, exact-degeneracy
  rotation invariance, reverse orders 1–3, JVP, jacfwd, vmap, 300 K and
  broader-temperature small-gap continuity, generalized-overlap finite
  differences, lane-specific masks, low-temperature Aufbau parity, and
  GFN2/NO2 channel-wise filling and partition parity.
- Functional map, solver, Fermi derivative, and filling selection: 152 passed.
- Canonical E0 spectral targets: 24 passed; no status-file changes.
- Focused E3.0 frozen NO2 spectral control (PyTorch): 2 passed.
- E3.1a non-slow root/IFT selection: 24 passed. The restored-512-node slow
  hyperpolarizability reference passed; its call took 442.93 s. The matching
  pol-deriv and NO2 dense-IFT slow rows passed in the bounded 256-node trial;
  512 nodes are the prior validated setting for those rows.
- NO2/GFN2 reference selection with libcint enabled: 8 passed, 2 expected
  XFAILs. The slowest third-order calls took 545.53 s (PyTorch) and 541.03 s
  (libcint). The stored references and `KNOWN_WRONG` markers are unchanged.
- Black, Python compilation, and `git diff --check` passed.

The bounded entropy-quadrature study compared 64, 128, 256, and 512
Gauss–Legendre nodes against 512 for exact finite-temperature degeneracy,
300 K and broader-temperature small gaps, generalized overlap, and a
wide-gap/root-tail case. On the synthetic derivative workload, total elapsed
times were 5.32, 5.63, 6.33, and 7.81 s respectively; a representative NO2
first-response evaluation took 1.17, 1.30, and 1.71 s at 128, 256, and 512
nodes. Although 256
matched the frozen NO2 matrix response closely, it widened the independent
hyperpolarizability physical-FD spread to `4.54e-4` in a clean repeat and
failed the unchanged `5e-4` plateau assertion in the full root-response
sequence. At 512, the corresponding clean spread was `2.69e-4` and the full
hyperpolarizability closure passed. The reduction was therefore rejected and
the production quadrature remains at 512 nodes.

Maximum candidate-versus-512 absolute differences over the recorded
value/reverse/JVP/jacfwd derivative vectors were `3.11e-8`, `4.95e-12`, and
`1.36e-11` for the 64-, 128-, and 256-node synthetic workloads respectively.
For the NO2 probe, the corresponding maximum differences were `3.41e-10` at
128 nodes and `1.10e-13` at 256 nodes. The real physical-FD closure, rather
than the isolated matrix comparison, therefore determines the retained 512
nodes.

The finalized observed timing baseline is `test_invariant_free_energy.py`:
141.56 s at 512 nodes; NO2 hyperpolarizability tight-root reference: 442.93 s;
NO2 `pol_deriv` tight-root reference: 336.68 s in the bounded candidate run;
NO2/GFN2 third-order reference calls: 545.53 s (PyTorch) and 541.03 s
(libcint). These are diagnostic/nightly workloads; the tight-root tests and
the NO2 dense-IFT case are marked `slow` without reducing their assertions.

E3.1s is complete. The next package is E3.1b, which attaches the physical-root
derivative to the accepted convergence-tested raw-map input. The NO2
finite-root diagnosis and historical markers remain unchanged.
