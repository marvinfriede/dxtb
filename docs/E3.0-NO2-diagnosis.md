# E3.0 diagnosis record: GFN2/NO2 third derivatives

E3.0 reproduced the current T0.2 failures for both baseline drivers. The
frozen-occupation, frozen-spectrum Fermi, one-map, and same-state term-set
probes do not reproduce the endpoint discrepancy; the first observed failing
boundary is the complete fixed-point/property response. The evidence does not
yet distinguish higher-order solver-trajectory composition from final
property/energy response. This record is diagnostic only; it does not change
the eigensolver, SCF mathematics, references, tolerances, or `KNOWN_WRONG`
entries. Any implementation change belongs to E3.1.

## Reproduction

The historical case is `SYSTEMS["NO2"]`, an open-shell doublet with `spin=1`.
The T0.2 helper uses `scf_mode="full"`, explicit Anderson mixing,
`maxiter=300`, `f_atol=x_atol=1e-10`, `verbosity=0`, float64 CPU, default
potential SCP mode, and the default 300 K Fermi settings. The method-wide
`KNOWN_WRONG[("NO2", "gfn2")]` applies to both PyTorch and libcint.

## Layer isolation

The tests in `test/test_baseline/test_e30_diagnostics.py` separately cover both
endpoint finite-difference cases on both drivers, the frozen-spectrum Fermi
response on both drivers, a fixed-occupation frozen-H/S spectral-density
probe on both drivers, a one-map potential-state probe on both drivers, a
same-GFN2-state interaction-set comparison, and a separate GFN1 no-dispersion
control. The full diagnostic selection passed 12 tests in the primary
environment.

At the converged GFN2/NO2 300 K spectrum, occupations are effectively integer:
the largest fractional occupation is `9.383e-12` for both drivers. The
frozen-spectrum Fermi probe perturbs the orbital energies along the same fixed
deterministic direction on each driver. Its nested third derivative is
`-6.43394e-7`; the finite differences at steps `(5e-3, 2.5e-3, 1.25e-3)` are
`(-6.81677e-7, -6.50147e-7, -6.53699e-7)` for each driver. The last two
steps agree within `3.5e-9`, and the nested result agrees with the stable
region within `1.1e-8`. This is a small, stable local Fermi response at the
converged spectrum; it does not characterize intermediate spectra along the
SCF trajectory.

With occupations frozen, a deterministic density projection through the
generalized eigensolver gives PyTorch nested third derivative `617.5608121`,
with finite differences `(622.9046994, 618.9104782, 617.8987210)` at steps
`(5e-3, 2.5e-3, 1.25e-3)`. The libcint nested result is `324.4069349`, with
finite differences `(332.1427211, 326.3579260, 324.8950341)` at the same
steps. Both approach their nested values in the last two steps and neither
reproduces either endpoint's failure.

For one raw potential map at the converged state, a deterministic potential
direction gives PyTorch nested third derivative `-1.0104171`; finite
differences at `5e-3` and `2.5e-3` are both `-1.0132790`. For libcint, the
nested value is `-1.1405647`; finite differences at `1.25e-2` and `1e-2` are
`-1.1429787` and `-1.1436641`. The libcint step pair is the stable region for
that driver's lower-precision integral evaluation. Both raw-map probes agree
with their stable finite-difference region within `0.01` and neither reproduces
the endpoint discrepancy.

A controlled term-set probe holds the GFN2 hcore, integrals, converged
potential, perturbation direction, and output projection fixed. It compares
the full GFN2 SCF interactions with the GFN1 interaction set (`ES2`/`ES3`) on
the identical monopole/dipole coordinates; the GFN2 quadrupole coordinate is
held fixed because GFN1 has no AES2 quadrupole channel. The full GFN2 map
projection has nested third derivative `-0.8722765` and finite differences
`(-0.8716590, -0.8716146, -0.8715830)` at `(1e-2, 7.5e-3, 5e-3)`. The
GFN1-term projection at that same GFN2 state has nested value `-1.0185504`
and finite differences `(-1.0191310, -1.0188674, -1.0186785)` at
`(2e-2, 1.5e-2, 1e-2)`. Each last-two-step pair is stable and agrees with
autograd within `0.001`. The term-set contrast changes the map's value/response
but neither variant reproduces the endpoint failure; it is not a full GFN1 vs
GFN2 energy comparison.

The GFN1 control used the same NO2 geometry, PyTorch driver, finite-temperature
and FULL-SCF settings, with `exclude=("disp",)` before setup. The resulting
System contains no `DispersionD3` component and resolves to the functional
FULL route. Its selected nested derivatives were `-3.8714616` and `0.8615611`,
while the direct energy finite differences converged to `-3.8713465` and
`0.8615548`. This control follows its finite differences, so the GFN2 failure
is not caused by the outer DFT-D3 term.

The current functional production configuration was checked separately:
FULL / potential / non-strict configured Broyden resolves to the functional
route with effective Anderson mixing. Its converged energy and spectral
outputs (charges, orbital energies, occupation, density, Hamiltonian, and
potential) agree with explicit Anderson to `5.4e-15` or better, with the same
20 iterations and convergence status. The selected third derivatives differed
by about `4.2e-3` for hyperpolarizability and `4.4e-4` for `pol_deriv`, far
below the corresponding nested-autograd versus finite-difference gaps; both
configurations retain the same failure. Coefficient tensors are eigenvector
gauge-dependent and were not used as parity evidence.

The first observed discrepancy in the tested ladder is therefore the
**complete fixed-point/property response**, after the local Fermi, frozen-H/S
spectral-density, one-raw-map, and same-state term-set probes. The endpoint
failure remains present with both PyTorch and libcint. The evidence does not
distinguish solver-trajectory higher-order composition from final
energy/property response, nor does the fixed-spectrum Fermi probe rule out a
different effect at intermediate trajectory spectra. E3.1 should investigate
that boundary and separately check whether the close occupied GFN2 orbital
pair contributes; this record does not assign that pair as the cause.
