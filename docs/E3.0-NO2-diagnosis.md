# E3.0 diagnosis: GFN2/NO2 third derivatives

sampled maps, fixed-count Anderson composition, adaptive convergence selection,
potential finalization, and public-property composition. No numerical,
SCF, reference, tolerance, or `KNOWN_WRONG` change is included here, and E3.1
has not started.

## NO2 trajectory spectra and local maps

The center PyTorch FULL/Anderson trajectory has 20 physical map evaluations.
For every evaluation the diagnostic records the map index, raw residual,
orbital energies, occupations, smallest adjacent/occupied pair gap, frontier
gap and whether the finite-temperature occupation changes materially. The
smallest occupied-pair gap occurs at zero-based map index 10 and is about
`0.00241637 Eh`; neighboring indices 8, 9 and 11 are about `0.00244 Eh`.
The frontier gap remains about `0.04825 Eh`, and the maximum fractional
occupation is about `9.39e-12`, so finite-temperature occupation response is
immaterial at these points.

At indices 8, 9, 10, 11 and the final index 19, the diagnostic freezes the
trajectory state and checks local raw-map response to physical field and
geometry perturbations. It checks one-map geometry/density response and
two-map field and geometry responses against derivative-level finite
differences. These sampled probes pass. At the smallest-gap state, the
ordinary no-grad forward spectrum (`broadening_method=None`) and
derivative-enabled Lorentzian branch have identical gauge-invariant density,
orbital energies and occupations at the center. These observations do not
prove every possible spectral input is correct; the canonical E0 small-gap
XFAILs remain open.

## Fixed-count Anderson and convergence accuracy

Eager early-stop and fixed-trip parity alone does not rule out convergence
selection: both production controllers use the same tensor convergence
predicate and selected/frozen trajectory. The stencil iterations do vary
(typically 20–24 for coarser steps), then settle at 20 for `h <= 5e-5`.
Therefore the diagnosis separately evaluates fixed-N Anderson functions,
with an always-active transition mask and exactly N physical map evaluations
at every perturbation. Third derivatives are compared with central finite
differences of the second derivative, and every stencil point is confirmed to
use the same N.

| Fixed map count N | Nested third derivative | Fixed-N FD of second derivative | Finding |
|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 18 | about `-872.960` | about `-3.35277` | disagreement |
| 20 | about `+0.02298` | about `-3.35277` | disagreement at historical center count |
| 22 | about `-3.41196` | about `-3.35277` | smaller but material disagreement |
| 24 | about `-3.35258` | about `-3.35277` | agrees within `1.9e-4` |

A fixed-count prefix audit finds agreement through prefixes 1–8, a sharp
third-derivative disagreement beginning at prefix 9, and agreement again by
prefix 24. This rules out *only* an input-dependent stop-count change as the
whole explanation: the fixed-N 20-map function itself disagrees with its own
derivative-level FD at the historical tolerance. The raw-map probes at the
sampled small-gap trajectory states pass, so current evidence points to the
composed Anderson/map trajectory near the close occupied pair, with finite
root accuracy also material. It does not identify a new defective spectral
formula or justify an implementation change by itself.

## Solver, finalization and property boundary

The split PyTorch tests use the same field/geometry perturbations and selected
solver setup. At the historical tolerance, both eager and fixed-trip
controllers report 20 center and stencil maps. Stage A (complete Anderson
trajectory to its selected pre-finalization map result) already has a third
derivative mismatch: field response is about `+0.023` against the stable FD
near `-3.35277`, and the mixed response is about `1.1502` against FD near
`0.7977`. First and second field derivatives remain within their recorded
agreement bounds. Stage B, `finalize_scf_potential()` on that exact outcome,
and stage C, the public Calculator property composition, retain the endpoint
failure. Thus finalization and the property wrapper are downstream of the
first observed mismatch; they are not its first onset.

| Boundary | Hyperpolarizability nested / FD | `pol_deriv` nested / FD | Result |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| A. Complete solver trajectory to selected pre-finalization map | about `+0.022` / `-3.35277` | about `1.1502` / `0.7977` | first observed mismatch |
| B. Potential finalization of the same solver outcome | about `33.8059` / `-3.35277` | about `4.6760` / `0.7977` | mismatch remains/amplifies |
| C. Public Calculator property composition | four driver/observable cases in endpoint table | four driver/observable cases in endpoint table | historical failure reproduced |

Eager and fixed-trip controls produce matching center outputs and similar
derivatives, but this parity is not evidence against convergence-selection
effects because both share the convergence predicate. The fixed-N tests above
separate that mechanism: there is a mismatch without adaptive selection at
N=20, and a strong improvement with more maps/tighter convergence. The
diagnosis is therefore a finite-root/composed-trajectory response problem,
not simply an eager Python `break` artifact.

Case B uses the same base T0.2 configuration, changing only to configured
non-strict Broyden, which resolves to the functional route with effective
Anderson. It compares energy, charges, orbital energies, occupation, density,
Hamiltonian, potential, convergence/iterations and both selected derivative
components; gauge-dependent coefficients are excluded. The committed test
uses Case A endpoints directly from the ordinary T0.2 `compute()` result, and
collects iteration evidence from a different fresh calculator.

## Diagnosis and validation

The evidence supports this current localization:

```text
sampled individual raw maps at the smallest-gap trajectory neighborhood: pass
fixed-N Anderson composition at N=20: derivative-level FD mismatch
adaptive/fixed-trip production solver at historical tolerance: mismatch already present
tighter convergence / N=24: third response approaches the FD plateau
potential finalization: preserves/amplifies the existing mismatch
public property composition: reproduces the historical endpoint failure
```

This distinguishes the observed result as a combined fixed-count trajectory
and finite-root-accuracy effect (possibility B with a substantial C
contribution); it does not claim the generic canonical small-gap eigensolver
defects are solved. The exact E0 spectral targets remain pre-existing
failures. E3.0 makes no selection of a numerical remedy; E3.1 remains
unstarted.

The current focused diagnostic tests assert the fixed-N N=18/20/22/24
characterization, the first failing prefix, tolerance-sweep convergence,
sampled intermediate-map agreement, all four solver/finalization boundary
cases, eager/fixed-trip center parity and the Case A/B route contract. The
canonical component selection above and the GFN2/NO2 reference selection are
separate required evidence. No references, tolerances, `KNOWN_WRONG` entries,
xfails, production SCF code, spectral implementation, or DFT-D3 code changed.
